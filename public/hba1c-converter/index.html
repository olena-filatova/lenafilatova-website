Questions

How do I convert HbA1c from % to mmol/mol?

Subtract 2.15 from the percentage and multiply by 10.929. So 7% is (7 − 2.15) × 10.929 = 53 mmol/mol. To go back, divide mmol/mol by 10.929 and add 2.15.

What average blood sugar does my HbA1c correspond to?

The ADAG study formula estimates average glucose (eAG) as 28.7 × HbA1c − 46.7 in mg/dL. An HbA1c of 7% (53 mmol/mol) corresponds to about 154 mg/dL, or 8.6 mmol/L. It is a population estimate: two people with the same HbA1c can have noticeably different averages.

How do I convert mmol/L to mg/dL?

Multiply mmol/L by 18 (18.016 exactly) to get mg/dL, and divide mg/dL by 18 to get mmol/L. For example, 5.5 mmol/L is 99 mg/dL and 180 mg/dL is 10.0 mmol/L.

What is a normal HbA1c?

In the UK, below 42 mmol/mol (6.0%) is the normal range, 42–47 mmol/mol (6.0–6.4%) means a higher risk of type 2 diabetes, and 48 mmol/mol (6.5%) or above is the level used to diagnose diabetes. A diagnosis is made by a clinician, usually with a repeat test. In the US, the ADA starts the prediabetes range lower, at 5.7% (39 mmol/mol).

What HbA1c should I aim for if I have diabetes?

NICE suggests 48 mmol/mol (6.5%) or lower for most adults with type 1 diabetes. For type 2 it suggests 48 mmol/mol when managed by diet or a single medicine that does not cause hypos, and 53 mmol/mol (7.0%) when on medicines that can. Your team may set a different target for you.

Why doesn't my CGM's GMI match my lab HbA1c?

GMI (glucose management indicator) is calculated only from your CGM's average glucose. HbA1c measures sugar attached to red blood cells over about three months, so anything that changes red-cell lifespan shifts it: iron-deficiency anaemia, recent blood loss, pregnancy, kidney disease or haemoglobin variants. A gap of half a percentage point or more is common.

What can make an HbA1c result misleading?

Iron-deficiency anaemia tends to push HbA1c up, which matters for women with heavy periods, including in perimenopause. Blood loss, a recent transfusion, haemolysis and pregnancy tend to push it down. If your HbA1c does not fit your finger-prick or CGM readings, ask your clinician whether one of these applies.
